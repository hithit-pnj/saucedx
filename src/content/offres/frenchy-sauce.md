    texte: >-
      A sparring partner knows the discipline and responds to your moves,
      instead of deciding them for you. That's the deal. You bring the texts
      you're working on and the questions you're stuck on; I bring the French,
      the context and a philosopher's ear. We read closely, test your
      formulations, and you do more of your own thinking in French.

      Together, we work towards two closely linked goals. The first is a precise
      command of written and academic French: we read the texts themselves,
      closely, and build the grammar from there rather than from drills. The
      second is ease and eloquence when you speak, because eloquence isn't about
      knowing everything, or sounding perfectly French; it's about sounding like
      yourself, in French. Expect rigour, and expect wit.

      - titre: What a session looks like
        texte: >-
          We start from a passage you're working on. You read it once, without
          stopping and without a dictionary. Then we take it apart together,
          until you understand it first-hand: what each clause is doing, which
          small word carries the argument, where the irony lies.

          When Foucault writes « Le structuralisme, ou du moins ce qu'on groupe
          sous ce nom un petit peu général », that « un petit peu » means: very
          general indeed, and he is about to step aside from it.

          Then you put the idea into your own French, out loud or on the page. I
          push back, ask the next question, and you find your own way to say it.
      - titre: What stays yours
        texte: >-

          chapter, translate it for you or tell you what your author really
          meant. I ask the questions that make you put it into words, point out
          what you might have missed, and give you feedback on what you write in
          French.

          You're the expert on your author; I bring the language, the context
          and a philosopher's ear.
